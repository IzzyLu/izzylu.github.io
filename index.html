01

Retinal electrophysiology

Stanford University · Vollrath Lab

PythonSignal analysisVisualization

Making ERG measurements
easier to inspect.

I developed a Python workflow to process electroretinography recordings, extract a- and b-wave measurements, and generate annotated plots and summary tables. An application interface makes the analysis accessible through file upload.

The central challenge was translating visual judgment into explicit analysis rules. I revised the scotopic peak-selection approach after reconsidering which positive peak represented the intended waveform feature.

InputInstrument text exports with recording metadata and voltage traces.
OutputResponse amplitudes and timing, marked peaks, plots, and tables.
Read the analysis approach

The workflow associates each trace with its recording conditions, uses a pre-stimulus baseline, and measures selected waveform features in defined time windows. Plots expose the selected points so an analyst can inspect the measurements.

The notebooks use different peak-selection rules for scotopic and photopic recordings. In the scotopic workflow, the implemented rule can select a later peak after the highest candidate; this is a temporal rule, not simply the second-highest peak by amplitude. The choice needs to be assessed against the recording protocol and waveform.

My contribution includes the analysis workflow, application interface, and revision of the peak-selection approach.

02

Quantitative microscopy

UC San Diego · Castle / Tuszynski Lab

QuPathImageJ / FijiImage analysis

Turning visual criteria
into a reusable workflow.

I developed a semiautomated QuPath and ImageJ workflow to support regional fluorescence quantification in AAV promoter studies. The work combined image processing, object detection, classification, and visual review.

A repeatable count is useful only if it captures the intended biological object. I refined detection and classification strategies while examining cases where fluorescent structures could be mistaken for cells.

InputFluorescence microscopy images and defined regions of interest.
OutputQuantification tables supported by inspectable detections and visual review.
Read the workflow approach

The workflow separates candidate-object detection from classification. That distinction matters when a classifier cannot recover an object that the detection step missed or segmented incorrectly.

I retained review of the original images and refined the procedure around failure cases. The contribution is the analysis workflow and its application to the research question; QuPath and ImageJ/Fiji are existing software developed by their respective communities.

Transfer to a new image set requires checking acquisition conditions, detection settings, and classification performance.

Research direction

Understanding regulation.
Building ways to control it.

My work on cortex-directed AAV promoters led me to questions about how regulatory sequences and cellular context shape gene expression.

I want to develop tools to study and control gene regulation and cellular function, combining experimental design with quantitative analysis. Across projects, I am interested in what makes a measurement interpretable and a method usable by someone else.

About me

I’m Isabella Lu, a Life Science Research Professional in the Vollrath Lab at Stanford University. I earned my BS in Human Biology and MS in Biology at UC San Diego, where I worked on AAV promoter development in the Castle / Tuszynski Lab.

My research spans experimental biology, microscopy, physiological measurements, and computational analysis. I enjoy connecting the practical limitations of an experiment to the tools needed to answer its underlying question.
